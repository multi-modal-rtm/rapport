\begin{abstract}
Graph-based context modeling is the dominant architectural paradigm in multimodal emotion recognition in conversation (ERC), yet reported gains are typically measured over frozen or weakly adapted encoders. We present a controlled attribution study asking whether conversation-graph machinery --- speaker-state recurrence, relationship memory, and shift-aware objectives --- retains measurable value once the text encoder is fine-tuned. Using a residual do-no-harm design in which every component provably initializes as an identity on a frozen foundation, we find that on frozen features the graph stack's paired gain is $+0.0356$ (std $0.0106$), reproducing the pattern reported in the literature. After the text encoder is fine-tuned, this gain no longer holds, even when the encoder is given no conversational context. The change between the two regimes is statistically robust: the difference-in-differences is $+0.0444$, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[+0.0347, +0.0560]$. It also holds for a second, larger encoder from a different family (DeBERTa-v3-large), where the difference-in-differences is $+0.0188$ and again excludes zero. Where the fine-tuned gain settles is not the same across encoders. For RoBERTa-base it is null to slightly negative ($-0.0088$, std $0.0072$, seven seeds); for DeBERTa-v3-large it is small but positive ($+0.0039$, three seeds), an order of magnitude below that encoder's own frozen-regime gain. Relational memory still improves an auxiliary shift-detection task on both corpora, showing the null is not from a broken component. We release the methodology, code, and pre-registered decision history as a reusable protocol.
\end{abstract}
